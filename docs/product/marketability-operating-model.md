# Viable Marketability Operating Model

## Purpose

Viable is a local-first marketability operating system. It helps a person or small team move a product from uncertain positioning to repeatable market learning, qualified demand, and sales support.

Event discovery is one source of opportunity evidence. Social conversations, search demand, website behavior, customer feedback, competitive movement, content performance, and sales outcomes are additional sources. No single provider owns the product model.

## The marketability loop

```text
Assess the product
  -> understand the market
  -> choose positioning and offers
  -> create useful assets
  -> distribute with approval
  -> capture and qualify demand
  -> support sales conversations
  -> measure outcomes
  -> improve the product and message
```

The loop is continuous. A campaign is not complete when content is published. It is complete when Viable can connect the work to evidence, outcomes, and a clear next decision.

## Marketability dimensions

Viable evaluates readiness through explicit dimensions rather than producing an unexplained magic score.

| Dimension | Core question | Evidence examples |
|---|---|---|
| Product truth | What does the product actually do today? | Product documentation, working features, limitations, claims ledger |
| Audience clarity | Who has the problem and who can buy? | ICPs, personas, roles, use cases, buying committees |
| Problem and urgency | Why should the audience act now? | Customer language, events, trends, pain signals, regulatory or operational triggers |
| Positioning | Why this product instead of alternatives or inaction? | Category, alternatives, differentiation, objections, competitive evidence |
| Offer readiness | What can a prospect evaluate or buy? | Packaging, pricing, trial, demo, onboarding, guarantee, call to action |
| Proof | Why should anyone believe the claims? | Case studies, testimonials, demonstrations, benchmarks, references, product evidence |
| Discoverability | Can the right people find it? | Search visibility, social presence, community presence, events, backlinks, listings |
| Content readiness | Can the product teach, persuade, and remain consistent? | Content pillars, asset inventory, editorial calendar, brand voice, reusable proof |
| Distribution readiness | Can approved content reach the intended audience reliably? | Connected channels, publishing rights, schedules, platform constraints |
| Conversion readiness | Can interest become a lead or next action? | Landing pages, forms, booking, lead magnets, calls to action, tracking |
| Sales readiness | Can a human conduct and advance the conversation? | One-pagers, discovery guides, demos, objection handling, proposals, follow-up |
| Measurement readiness | Can outcomes be attributed and learned from? | Analytics, campaign IDs, UTM rules, lead source, pipeline stages, experiments |

Each dimension must expose its evidence, confidence, gaps, recommendations, owner, and last-reviewed date.

## Product systems

### 1. Product truth and claims

Viable maintains the product record that all downstream work uses.

The product record includes:

- product name, category, lifecycle stage, and release state;
- capabilities, limitations, dependencies, and supported environments;
- approved claims and prohibited or unverified claims;
- pricing, packaging, offers, calls to action, and buying path;
- proof assets and evidence references;
- brand voice, terminology, visual rules, and accessibility expectations;
- target audiences, buyer roles, users, influencers, and disqualifiers.

Generated content must reference this record rather than inventing capabilities from a prompt.

### 2. Market and opportunity intelligence

Viable gathers evidence about demand and market movement from supported public or authorized sources.

Sources may include:

- event listings and calendars;
- public social posts and feeds;
- search queries, search performance, and content gaps;
- competitor websites, release notes, pricing pages, and public announcements;
- communities, newsletters, podcasts, videos, reviews, and public discussions;
- customer interviews, sales notes, support themes, and imported research;
- industry news, standards, policy changes, and partnership opportunities.

The system normalizes evidence into signals, topics, questions, objections, organizations, people, opportunities, and recommended actions.

### 3. Positioning and campaign strategy

Viable converts evidence into a deliberate plan.

Strategy capabilities include:

- ICP and persona definition;
- jobs, pains, desired outcomes, triggers, and objections;
- category and alternative analysis;
- value proposition and message hierarchy;
- campaign objective, audience, offer, proof, call to action, and success metric;
- channel selection based on audience fit rather than platform popularity;
- launch plans, evergreen plans, event-driven plans, and account-focused plans;
- risk, claim, brand, and accessibility review.

### 4. Content and creative studio

Viable creates and manages evidence-backed content packages.

Supported asset families should include:

- website pages and landing pages;
- blog posts, articles, guides, FAQs, comparison pages, and case studies;
- LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, X, Threads, Bluesky, and Mastodon drafts;
- newsletters and lifecycle email;
- sales emails, direct-message drafts, follow-up sequences, and call notes;
- one-pagers, battlecards, pitch decks, demo scripts, proposals, and launch kits;
- image briefs, carousels, diagrams, thumbnails, and ad concepts;
- short-video scripts, hooks, shot lists, storyboards, captions, voiceover copy, and edit instructions;
- reusable content atoms that can be adapted without losing source meaning.

Every asset retains its campaign, audience, claim, evidence, channel, owner, approval state, version, and performance history.

### 5. Website, SEO, AEO, and conversion

Viable treats the website as a conversion product, not an online brochure abandoned after launch.

Capabilities include:

- site and content inventory;
- page-purpose and audience mapping;
- technical search checks for crawlability, indexability, metadata, canonical URLs, sitemaps, structured data, mobile usability, performance, and accessibility;
- keyword, question, topic, and content-cluster planning;
- page briefs and copy review for clarity, differentiation, proof, and calls to action;
- internal-link and content-refresh recommendations;
- answer-engine readiness through clear facts, direct answers, structured sections, authorship, citations, and consistent product entities;
- landing-page and form review;
- conversion-path mapping and friction detection;
- experiment proposals and result tracking.

Viable should integrate with first-party search and analytics sources where authorized, while preserving a manual import path.

### 6. Channel distribution and publishing

Channel adapters translate an approved canonical asset into platform-specific payloads.

The core owns:

- the canonical asset;
- audience and campaign intent;
- approvals;
- scheduling intent;
- destination identity;
- disclosure requirements;
- delivery evidence;
- retry and failure state.

The adapter owns:

- authorization;
- platform formatting and media constraints;
- API calls;
- rate-limit handling;
- platform response normalization.

Initial channel targets are LinkedIn, Facebook Pages, Instagram professional accounts, X, TikTok, YouTube, Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon, website CMS platforms, and email providers. Each adapter must pass a current access, pricing, scope, policy, and app-review assessment before implementation.

### 7. Lead capture and relationship management

Viable connects marketing activity to people, organizations, and opportunities without pretending every social interaction is a sales lead.

Capabilities include:

- forms, booking links, newsletter subscriptions, event contacts, and manual lead capture;
- consent, source, campaign, and attribution records;
- people and organization records with evidence-backed deduplication;
- lifecycle stage, qualification, owner, next action, and conversation history;
- lead scoring with visible factors and confidence;
- reminders and follow-up queues;
- CRM synchronization through optional adapters;
- suppression, unsubscribe, do-not-contact, and retention controls.

### 8. Sales enablement

Viable gives a seller or founder the context and material needed to conduct a useful conversation.

Capabilities include:

- account and contact briefs;
- discovery questions;
- meeting preparation;
- product and industry talking points;
- objection and alternative analysis;
- tailored demonstrations and proof selection;
- follow-up drafts;
- one-pagers, proposals, mutual action plans, and handoff summaries;
- win, loss, stall, and objection capture that feeds the marketability loop.

### 9. Measurement and learning

Viable connects work to outcomes through a common measurement model.

The measurement system includes:

- campaign, asset, channel, audience, offer, and experiment identifiers;
- reach, impressions, engagement, clicks, visits, conversions, qualified leads, meetings, opportunities, revenue, and retention outcomes where available;
- cost and effort records;
- attribution with explicit model and uncertainty;
- content and channel benchmarks;
- experiment hypotheses, variants, guardrails, and decisions;
- recommendations grounded in observed results;
- a learning ledger that records what changed and why.

## Product journeys

### First-run journey

1. Create or import a product workspace.
2. Verify product capabilities, limitations, claims, and proof.
3. Define audiences, buyers, users, and disqualifiers.
4. Connect or import website, analytics, search, social, event, and CRM evidence.
5. Run the marketability assessment.
6. Review the evidence behind each score and recommendation.
7. Select a priority outcome and create the first campaign.

### Weekly operating journey

1. Review new market, event, social, search, and performance signals.
2. Accept, dismiss, tag, or connect signals to products and campaigns.
3. Review the prioritized marketability gaps.
4. Plan campaigns and content for the next period.
5. Create and approve assets.
6. Schedule or export approved assets.
7. Review leads, follow-ups, pipeline movement, and experiments.
8. Record decisions and update product truth where evidence changed.

### Launch journey

1. Confirm product and offer readiness.
2. Define launch audiences, messages, proof, channels, and success measures.
3. Build the launch asset matrix.
4. Complete website, search, analytics, and conversion checks.
5. Prepare publication and outreach queues.
6. Run approval and claim checks.
7. Publish or export through authorized adapters.
8. Monitor conversations, leads, failures, and performance.
9. Produce a launch retrospective and prioritized follow-up plan.

## Safety, privacy, and trust boundaries

Viable must not:

- invent product capabilities, customer evidence, or market facts;
- scrape private content or bypass platform access controls;
- store tokens, passwords, cookies, or webhooks in repository content or plaintext reports;
- infer sensitive personal traits for targeting;
- send outreach solely because an automated score crossed a threshold;
- create fake engagement, impersonation, undisclosed endorsements, or deceptive social proof;
- publish regulated, high-risk, or unsupported claims without review;
- conceal partial failures behind empty results;
- claim attribution certainty that the evidence does not support;
- allow a delivery adapter to approve its own content.

## Success definition

Viable succeeds when a user can answer these questions with evidence:

1. Is this product ready to market?
2. What is preventing it from being easier to discover, understand, trust, try, and buy?
3. Which audience and opportunity should receive attention now?
4. What should we say, create, publish, or improve next?
5. Which work produced useful conversations, qualified demand, sales progress, or product learning?
6. What should change in the product, message, channel mix, or offer because of the results?
