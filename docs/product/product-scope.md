# Viable Product Scope

## Product definition

Viable is a local-first marketability operating system for products, public repositories, and small product teams.

It helps a user determine whether a product is ready to market, identify the most valuable audience and opportunity, create evidence-backed campaigns and assets, coordinate approved distribution, capture qualified demand, support sales conversations, and learn from outcomes.

## Core product domains

### Product truth

- capabilities and limitations;
- audiences and buying roles;
- claims and proof;
- positioning and alternatives;
- pricing, packaging, offers, and calls to action;
- brand voice and terminology;
- asset inventory and maturity.

### Marketability assessment

- transparent readiness dimensions;
- supporting evidence and confidence;
- prioritized gaps;
- recommended actions;
- ownership, effort, dependency, and verification.

### Market intelligence

- event signals;
- social and community signals;
- search and website signals;
- competitor and category signals;
- public repository signals;
- customer, support, and sales feedback;
- research inbox and evidence relationships.

### Campaign strategy

- audience and outcome;
- problem, trigger, offer, and message;
- proof and call to action;
- channel strategy;
- asset matrix;
- schedule and measurement;
- review and approval.

### Content and creative production

- website and search content;
- social content;
- articles, guides, comparisons, FAQs, and case studies;
- email and outreach drafts;
- sales enablement assets;
- image and carousel briefs;
- short-video briefs, scripts, storyboards, and production packages;
- review, versioning, approval, and asset history.

### Activation

- editorial and campaign calendar;
- manual exports;
- approved platform adapters;
- publication and delivery evidence;
- lead capture;
- follow-up queues;
- public repository launch rooms.

### Leads and sales support

- people and organizations;
- source and consent;
- qualification and lifecycle;
- account research;
- meeting preparation;
- objection handling;
- follow-up and proposals;
- outcome and learning capture.

### Measurement and learning

- campaign and asset performance;
- website and search outcomes;
- public repository growth;
- lead and opportunity influence;
- experiments;
- attribution with stated uncertainty;
- retrospectives and learning ledger.

## Integration scope

Viable may integrate with:

- websites and content management systems;
- analytics and search platforms;
- social research and publishing platforms;
- public event providers;
- GitHub and package ecosystems;
- email, newsletter, booking, and CRM systems;
- design, image, audio, and video tools;
- local model and automation runtimes.

Integrations remain adapters. They do not own canonical product, campaign, evidence, approval, identity, lead, or asset records.

## Explicit non-goals

Viable is not:

- an autonomous social persona;
- a bulk messaging or spam platform;
- a fake-engagement or paid-star system;
- a general-purpose CRM replacement;
- a social listening surveillance product;
- a guarantee of search ranking, GitHub Trending, virality, or sales;
- a compliance control plane;
- a replacement for legal, privacy, advertising, accessibility, or platform-policy review;
- a reason to bypass supported APIs, access controls, consent, or licensing.

## Product authority

Viable owns:

- the product model;
- marketability assessment;
- evidence and provenance;
- campaign intent;
- canonical assets and variants;
- claims and proof relationships;
- review and approval state;
- lead and relationship context;
- measurement and learning records.

External providers own their APIs, accounts, delivery systems, platform responses, and service availability.

## Success criteria

Viable is successful when a user can answer with evidence:

1. Is the product ready to market?
2. What is preventing discovery, understanding, trust, evaluation, or purchase?
3. Which audience and opportunity deserve attention now?
4. What should be created, improved, published, or discussed next?
5. What requires human approval?
6. What produced qualified interest, sales progress, adoption, contribution, or product learning?
7. What should change because of the observed results?
