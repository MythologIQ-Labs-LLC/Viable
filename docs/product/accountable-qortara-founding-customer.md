## 1. Purpose

This document establishes Accountable as the first named customer validation engagement managed through Viable for Qortara SDLC.

The designation has two linked purposes:

1. deliver a commercially useful Qortara SDLC subscription to Accountable; and
2. use Viable to preserve the product truth, customer hypothesis, offer, evidence, activation, outcome, and learning required to determine whether Qortara has a repeatable market.

Accountable is the founding customer, not permission to confuse a friendly relationship with validated product-market fit. The engagement must produce explicit evidence of useful adoption, buyer authority, willingness to pay, delivery cost, recurring value, retention potential, and reusable onboarding.

## 2. Governing customer promise

The governing customer promise remains the Qortara delivery contract:

> Accountable can continue experimenting with products, pods, AI, and delivery models while Qortara continuously preserves a trustworthy map of system ownership, customer-data exposure, release evidence, unresolved risk, and product drift.

Viable may record, assess, position, package, activate, and learn from this promise. Viable must not silently rewrite the Qortara delivery contract or claim outcomes unsupported by customer evidence.

The authoritative first-customer delivery contract is `docs/product/first-customer-mvp-accountable.md` in Qortara SDLC PR #47.

### 3.2 Viable owns marketability and learning truth

Viable owns the internal operating record for:

- Qortara product truth and supported claims;

- buyer, decision-maker, user, champion, influencer, blocker, and disqualifier roles;
- offer and packaging hypotheses;
- validation experiments;
- approved customer-facing assets and messages;

- commercial and adoption outcomes;
- retrospectives and learning-ledger entries;
- the reversible next action.

Viable is not the customer evidence store for Accountable production systems and is not the runtime control plane for Qortara.

### 3.3 Accountable retains customer authority

Accountable retains authority over:

- its product direction;
- App 4, Accountable OS, App 3, and fleet-docs responsibilities;
- customer-data processing and production operations;
- release, migration, and retirement decisions;
- risk acceptance;
- commercial approval;
- access granted to Qortara;
- remediation priorities and separately billed work.

Neither Viable nor Qortara may replace Ian or another authorized Accountable owner as product authority.

## 4. Founding-customer designation

Accountable is designated as Qortara SDLC's first founding-customer candidate immediately.

This designation means:

- Accountable is the first named organization for which a complete external-adopter subscription contract is defined;
- the engagement is the first end-to-end test of Qortara tenant provisioning, entitlement, evidence collection, posture delivery, reporting, and customer operation;
- Accountable-specific needs must be classified without contaminating shared Qortara semantics;
- the engagement must produce a reusable second-customer onboarding model;
- Viable must preserve both positive and negative evidence from the engagement.

This designation does not yet prove:

- a signed commercial agreement;
- payment;
- successful onboarding;
- recurring customer value;
- retention;
- product-market fit;
- a generally available Qortara release.

Those states require separate evidence and must not be inferred from this document, repository access, personal trust, or implementation activity.

## 5. Product truth baseline

The product being validated is a managed Qortara SDLC subscription with these entitled capabilities:

- Qortara SDLC workspace and external-adopter evidence path;
- Qor Oversight read-only governance posture;
- Qor Compliance evidence mapping and export;
- exact identity, revision, digest, source authority, freshness, and receipt preservation;
- customer-specific policy configuration without customer-specific core logic;
- weekly governance posture reporting;
- monthly compliance evidence delivery;
- product-shape drift detection;
- owner-action, acknowledgement, exception, and risk-acceptance workflows.

The subscription is read-only by default. Application development, database remediation, security implementation, App 3 migration execution, release management, legal advice, certification, and unlimited incident response remain separately scoped services.

## 6. Initial customer and ICP hypothesis

### 6.1 Candidate segment

The initial ICP hypothesis is:

> Founder-led or small software organizations operating multiple evolving applications, repositories, data stores, AI-assisted delivery paths, and customer operations, where system authority, privacy exposure, release evidence, and product shape are changing faster than the organization can reliably govern them.

Accountable is a strong founding-customer candidate because it currently exhibits:

- multiple applications with different product and operational roles;
- an active legacy retirement problem;
- evolving product shape and unclear future application boundaries;
- customer-data, consent, lifecycle, communication, and billing exposure;
- AI-assisted development and cross-repository delivery;
- a need for useful governance without surrendering product authority;
- a practical need for recurring evidence rather than a one-time document review.

### 6.2 Problem hypothesis

The target problem is not merely code quality. It is the inability to maintain a trustworthy, current, evidence-bound understanding of:

- what systems exist;
- which system owns each important fact or action;
- what changed and at which exact revision;
- what customer data and authority were affected;
- what risk remains unresolved;
- whether a release, migration, product pivot, or retirement claim is supported by evidence.

### 6.3 Desired outcome

Accountable should be able to change product direction without losing control of system ownership, customer-data exposure, release provenance, unresolved risk, or retirement evidence.

The desired operational outcomes are:

- faster understanding of current system and release state;
- fewer unresolved or accidental authority conflicts;
- evidence-backed App 3 retirement;
- explicit product-shape drift decisions;
- repeatable weekly governance operation;
- reproducible monthly compliance evidence;
- clear separation between subscription value and remediation work.

| Economic buyer | Ian or Accountable's authorized budget owner | Requires commercial confirmation |

| Primary user | Ian and authorized Accountable operators | Requires onboarding confirmation |

| Security or privacy approver | Accountable-designated owner | Unresolved |
| Risk-acceptance authority | Accountable-designated owner | Unresolved |

Viable must preserve role uncertainty. Familiarity with the customer does not prove buying authority, urgency, budget, or successful adoption.

## 10. Validation experiments

### Experiment 1: Buyer and urgency validation

Hypothesis: Accountable recognizes the governance and evidence problem as urgent enough to fund.

Success evidence:

- an authorized buyer confirms the problem and intended outcome;
- commercial authority and budget path are explicit;
- a decision date is recorded;
- the customer accepts the subscription versus remediation boundary.

Failure or inconclusive evidence:

- interest without authority or budget;
- no decision timeline;
- value framed only as Kevin's internal support work;
- expectation of unlimited remediation inside the subscription.

### Experiment 2: Minimum evidence access

Hypothesis: Accountable can grant sufficient read-only evidence access for useful onboarding without granting production mutation authority.

Success evidence:

- the initial repositories are registered;
- exact revisions and source identity are preserved;
- missing sources remain visible;
- adapter disablement does not affect Accountable production.

Failure or inconclusive evidence:

- access is too incomplete to support the customer promise;
- evidence requires unsafe credentials or mutation authority;
- customer value depends on undocumented manual access.

### Experiment 3: Time to first value

Hypothesis: the initial system map, authority map, action queue, App 3 retirement inventory, and evidence package create meaningful value within the agreed onboarding window.

Success evidence:

- Ian can identify what exists, who owns what, where risk is concentrated, and what requires a decision;
- at least one previously unclear authority, drift, security, privacy, or retirement condition becomes actionable;
- the customer reports reduced time required to understand product and release state.

### Experiment 4: Recurring value

Hypothesis: weekly posture and monthly evidence delivery remain useful after baseline discovery.

Success evidence:

- new or changed evidence affects customer attention or decisions;
- stale and unresolved state remains visible;
- the customer continues using reports and reviews;
- renewal or continued payment is supported by observed value.

Failure evidence:

- recurring output merely repeats the baseline;
- reports are ignored;
- support effort exceeds viable subscription economics;
- the customer only values remediation labor.

### Experiment 5: Reusability

Hypothesis: the Accountable engagement produces a generic second-customer onboarding model.

Success evidence:

- customer-specific logic remains in versioned policy and configuration;
- external-adopter contracts remain provider-neutral where practical;
- onboarding, entitlement, evidence, reporting, and offboarding are reusable;
- Accountable-specific professional services are clearly separated;
- a second-customer profile can be created without changing shared Qortara semantics.

## 11. Commercial and product success criteria

The founding-customer validation succeeds only when all required delivery acceptance criteria in Qortara PR #47 are met and Viable can record evidence for:

- an authorized commercial decision;
- agreed price and scope;
- signed or otherwise durable customer acceptance;
- successful read-only onboarding;
- first-value delivery;
- recurring operational use;
- actual delivery effort and support cost;
- customer feedback and unresolved objections;
- payment or an explicit approved founding-customer commercial arrangement;
- a documented renewal, continuation, expansion, or stop decision;
- reusable learning for the second customer.

Product-market fit is not an acceptance criterion for one customer. One engagement can validate a customer problem, offer, workflow, and willingness to pay. It cannot prove a market by itself.

## 12. Privacy, security, and customer-data restrictions

Viable must not receive or retain Accountable production customer records merely to manage this engagement.

The Viable operating record may contain:

- organization and stakeholder names approved for the engagement;
- product, offer, decision, activation, outcome, and learning records;
- links or immutable references to approved Qortara evidence;
- bounded commercial notes;
- explicit provenance, confidence, freshness, and limitations.

The Viable operating record must not contain:

- Accountable credentials or secrets;
- copied production datasets;
- member PII unrelated to the commercial relationship;
- private repository content beyond approved references or bounded evidence;
- legal conclusions or unsupported compliance claims;
- customer testimonials, logos, or case-study claims without consent.

Qortara remains the governed evidence system for the subscribed service. Viable references delivery evidence without duplicating sensitive source material.

## 13. Learning classification

Every Accountable request or discovered need must be classified as one of:

- generic Qortara capability;
- reusable external-adopter configuration;
- versioned Accountable policy;
- Viable product or workflow improvement;
- customer-only professional service;
- commercial packaging change;
- rejected scope expansion;
- unresolved decision.

This classification must occur before implementation is treated as product roadmap evidence.
